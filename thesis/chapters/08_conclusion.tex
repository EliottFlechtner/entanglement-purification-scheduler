\section{Summary}
\label{sec:conc:summary}

\glsreset{dag}
\glsreset{hrgs}
\glsreset{rgs}

This thesis formalized the schedule space left informal by the source papers~\cite{Benchasattabuse2026Bridging,Benchasattabuse2025Integrating} and searched it systematically, yielding four contributions.

\begin{enumerate}

      \item \emph{A validated formal model.} A non-adaptive purification schedule is represented as a \Gls{dag} $\Sigma=(T,\phi)$ and evaluated by a single bottom-up pass, which reproduces the source paper's fidelity results to four significant figures (Section~\ref{sec:exp:validation}). This provides a common representation in which different purification patterns, copy counts, and heralding placements can be described and evaluated under the same physical model.

      \item \emph{A three-tier search framework.} At the source papers' reference configuration, the optimizer improves rate over the paper's own hand-picked schedule both at matched cost and, more substantially, once the budget is relaxed (Section~\ref{sec:exp:headline}). Since that hand-picked schedule is explicitly presented by its authors as one demonstration example rather than an optimized design, this comparison mainly aims to show that the search mechanisms produce real working schedules. The framework also allows the objective and resource constraints to be changed without modifying the underlying candidate construction and evaluation procedure, making the same search machinery applicable to the different optimization questions considered in this thesis.

      \item \emph{A feasibility advantage under non-ideal noise.} Under inner-qubit error the source papers do not model, the paper's fixed schedule can fall below $F\ge0.9$ entirely while a searched schedule at the same cost remains feasible (Section~\ref{sec:exp:netgen}). This feasibility advantage, rather than the rate advantage, is the strongest result of the thesis, and it extends beyond hand-picked configurations, appearing both across 100 random per-hop heterogeneous networks and in a deliberately constructed challenging case (Section~\ref{sec:exp:heterogeneous}). The result illustrates why schedule optimization becomes more relevant when different parts of a chain experience different noise conditions: a fixed uniform construction cannot redistribute purification effort in response to those conditions, where optimized searched schedules can.

      \item \emph{A synthesis of when scheduling matters.} The advantage is largest when the fidelity constraint is restrictive enough to differentiate schedules, but not so restrictive that no searched schedule remains feasible. This intermediate regime appears both as noise increases and as the chain length grows, while the benefit is smaller when the fidelity constraint is easily satisfied (Chapter~\ref{chap:discussion}). The timing experiments further show that the preferred heralding strategy depends on the balance between communication delay, memory dephasing, and purification success probability, rather than being an intrinsic property of the purification operation itself.

\end{enumerate}

\clearpage

These results should be interpreted as existence claims within the searched schedule families, not proofs of global optimality over the full legal schedule space (Section~\ref{sec:disc:scope}). The finite beam width and restricted construction families \emph{prevent concluding that no better schedule exists}; the excluded-construction experiment illustrates this directly, as a feasible schedule outside the searched families outperforms the best schedule found within them.

Taken together, the results support this thesis's working hypothesis (Section~\ref{sec:intro:gap}): systematic search can improve on the paper's hand-picked baseline at equal cost and, more importantly, retain feasibility where that baseline fails. The key implication is that treating purification scheduling as an optimization problem can reveal useful constructions that fixed uniform design rules may miss.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:conc:future}

The results also point to several extensions that would broaden the scope of the optimizer and reduce the gap between the current model and a deployable network.

\begin{itemize}

      \item \emph{Adaptive scheduling.} Conditioning later purification decisions on earlier measurement or heralding outcomes, within a single execution, could adapt to the realized state of the network rather than committing to a fixed sequence in advance. This would extend the current non-adaptive schedule space and could avoid spending resources on purification steps that become unnecessary after an intermediate outcome. Reinforcement learning is one candidate approach, though the state and action spaces would need to be redefined carefully for this setting.

      \item \emph{Online optimization.} Rather than adapting within one execution, the search itself could be re-run periodically as noise and link quality drift over minutes to months, instead of being fixed once at commissioning as it is throughout this thesis. This would allow the selected schedule to follow changes in physical conditions, particularly in heterogeneous networks where different hops may degrade at different rates.

      \item \emph{Multi-path routing.} Extending the \Gls{dag} formalism from a single repeater chain to tree or mesh networks would require a more general connectivity representation and a corresponding extension of the current span-based search. In such networks, purification scheduling and route selection would become coupled decisions: the optimizer could potentially choose not only where and when to purify, but also which physical path or set of paths should be used to establish the end-to-end pair.

      \item \emph{Beyond the Pauli-channel model.} Hardware-specific noise models, including more detailed photon-loss and gate-error processes, would let the optimizer be evaluated against a broader range of physical implementations and could change the relative ranking of candidate schedules.

\end{itemize}

Overall, this work provides a starting point for systematic purification-schedule optimization. Even within a restricted non-adaptive space, purification placement and allocation affect both rate and the ability to meet a target fidelity. Extending the approach to adaptive decisions, time-varying networks, richer topologies, and more realistic noise models would test whether these gains persist under more realistic conditions.

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:conc:summary}

\glsreset{dag}
\glsreset{hrgs}
\glsreset{rgs}

This thesis formalized the schedule space left informal by the source papers~\cite{Benchasattabuse2026Bridging,Benchasattabuse2025Integrating} and searched it systematically, yielding four contributions.

\begin{enumerate}

      \item \emph{A validated formal model.} A non-adaptive purification schedule is represented as a \Gls{dag} $\Sigma=(T,\phi)$ and evaluated by a single bottom-up pass, which reproduces the source paper's fidelity results to four significant figures (Section~\ref{sec:exp:validation}).

      \item \emph{A three-tier search framework.} At the source papers' reference configuration, the optimizer improves rate over the paper's own hand-picked schedule both at matched cost and, more substantially, once the budget is relaxed (Section~\ref{sec:exp:headline}). Since that hand-picked schedule is explicitly presented by its authors as one example rather than an optimized design, this comparison mainly demonstrates that the search machinery produces real, working schedules.

      \item \emph{A feasibility advantage under non-ideal noise.} Under inner-qubit error the source papers do not model, the paper's fixed schedule can fall below $F\ge0.9$ entirely while a searched schedule at the same cost remains feasible (Section~\ref{sec:exp:netgen}). This feasibility advantage, rather than rate advantage, is the strongest result of the thesis, and it holds beyond hand-picked configurations, both on average over 100 random per-hop heterogeneous networks and on one deliberately constructed one (Section~\ref{sec:exp:heterogeneous}).

      \item \TOFIX{rephrase after chapter 7}{\emph{A synthesis of when scheduling matters.} The advantage is largest in an intermediate regime, tight enough that placement decisions bind but not so tight that no searched schedule is feasible; the same regime recurs across noise level and chain length (Chapter~\ref{chap:discussion}).}

\end{enumerate}

These are existence claims within the searched schedule families, not proofs of global optimality over the full legal schedule space (Section~\ref{sec:disc:scope}). Together they support this thesis's working hypothesis (Section~\ref{sec:intro:gap}): systematic search improves on the paper's hand-picked baseline at equal cost and, more importantly, retains feasibility in regimes where that baseline fails.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:conc:future}

\begin{itemize}

      \item \emph{Adaptive scheduling.} Conditioning later purification decisions on earlier measurement or heralding outcomes, within a single execution, could adapt to the realized state of the network rather than committing to a fixed sequence in advance; reinforcement learning is one candidate approach, though the state and action space would need to be defined carefully for this setting.

      \item \emph{Online optimization.} Rather than adapting within one execution, the search itself could be re-run periodically as noise and link quality drift over minutes to months, instead of being fixed once at commissioning as it is throughout this thesis.

      \item \emph{Multi-path routing.} Extending the \Gls{dag} formalism from a single repeater chain to tree or mesh networks would require a more general connectivity representation and a corresponding extension of the current span-based search.

      \item \emph{Beyond the Pauli-channel model.} Hardware-specific noise models, including more detailed photon-loss and gate-error processes, would let the optimizer be evaluated against a broader range of physical implementations and could change the relative ranking of candidate schedules.

\end{itemize}

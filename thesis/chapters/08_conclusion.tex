\section{Summary}
\label{sec:conc:summary}

This work makes four concrete contributions to the problem of purification schedule optimization for HRGS-based quantum repeaters.

\begin{itemize}
      \item \emph{A validated formal model.} A fixed, non-adaptive purification schedule is represented as a single directed acyclic graph $\Sigma = (T,\phi)$, evaluated by one bottom-up pass. Applied to the three canonical schedule types, this evaluator reproduces the source paper's fidelity figures to four significant figures (0.8234/0.9168/0.9295).
      \item \emph{A three-tier search framework, demonstrated at the paper's own reference configuration.} At matched cost ($C=100$) the optimizer achieves +2.5\% rate over the paper's schedule. The default budget-relaxed search spends $C=60$ for +52.8\% rate; the same search with the same-span pumping move disabled instead reaches $C=50$, $R=6713.18$, the historical headline figure reported before that move was included in the search.
      \item \emph{A feasibility advantage under non-ideal noise.} Network generalization experiments show that the paper's static schedule fails the $F \ge 0.9$ floor under non-ideal inner-qubit error (dropping as low as $F=0.6737$), while a searched schedule restores feasibility within the same $C=100$ budget. This is the strongest result in the thesis: a \emph{feasibility} advantage, not merely a rate-percentage improvement.
      \item \emph{Five named design findings, with specific empirical anchors.} In particular, within the searched families the minimum feasible budget over $N=10$--28 follows a descriptive fit of $E_{\max}^{\min} \approx 1.369\,N^{1.620}$, a super-linear trend that places the paper's linear $10N$ formula under pressure near $N=28$.
\end{itemize}

All results above are validated existence claims within the searched schedule families, not proofs of global optimality (Section~\ref{sec:disc:scope}).

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:conc:future}

Four directions stand out as natural extensions.

\begin{itemize}
      \item \emph{Adaptive scheduling.} A policy that conditions each purification step on the previous measurement outcome would likely outperform any fixed schedule; reinforcement learning is one natural approach, but the action and state spaces need careful definition.
      \item \emph{Online optimization.} As network conditions drift (noise, link quality), a schedule that was optimal at commissioning time may no longer be; integrating the search with real-time noise estimation would address this.
      \item \emph{Multi-path routing.} The current model assumes a single repeater chain; extending the DAG formalism to tree or mesh topologies would require generalizing the span partial order.
      \item \emph{Beyond the Pauli channel.} Replacing the Pauli-channel assumption with hardware-specific noise characterisation (e.g., from photon-loss-dominated or gate-error-dominated devices) would make the optimizer directly applicable to real hardware targets.
\end{itemize}

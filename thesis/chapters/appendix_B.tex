\section{Extended Physics Validation}
\label{app:validation}

Section~\ref{sec:exp:validation} reports the evaluator's agreement with the fidelity results of the Integrating paper's Fig.~5~\cite{Benchasattabuse2025Integrating} when varying the decoherence parameter $e_d$. Table~\ref{tab:app:validation} and Figure~\ref{fig:app:validation} provide the evaluator's fidelity output for the raw, heralded-pumping, and optimistic-pumping canonical schedules (Section~\ref{sec:bg:decoherence}) across the full range $e_d \in [0, 0.01]$ used throughout Chapter~\ref{chap:experiments}.

The source paper's Fig.~5 also covers this full $e_d$ range, and Figure~\ref{fig:app:validation} reproduces its shape throughout. The paper reports numeric fidelity values at only two points on the curve, $e_d=0.000$ and $e_d=0.010$: approximately $\{1.000, 1.000, 1.000\}$ and $\{0.823, 0.917, 0.929\}$, respectively, for the same three schedules in the same order. The evaluator matches both published points to at least three significant figures, and to four at $e_d=0.010$ (already reported in Section~\ref{sec:exp:validation} and shown in bold in Table~\ref{tab:app:validation}). The remaining nine rows trace the same reproduced curve and are included for completeness.

\input{tables/weak_link_contrast}

Figure~\ref{fig:app:heterogeneity} shows the complete 100-network random sample. Each point gives the optimizer's rate improvement over the best feasible uniform link-level recipe as a function of the network's per-hop noise heterogeneity (measured by the coefficient of variation (i.e.\ the standard deviation divided by the mean inner-qubit error rate across hops). The largest improvements occur in networks with relatively modest noise dispersion, where a single noisier hop leaves the remaining hops sufficiently inexpensive for the optimizer to reallocate resources towards the weak link.

\section{Timing Sensitivity, Full Sweeps}
\label{app:timing}

Section~\ref{sec:exp:timing} examines the effects of memory dephasing and generation latency on schedule rate and fidelity. Figure~\ref{fig:timing} shows both sweeps in full at $N=10$ and $e_d=0.01$.

\paragraph{Memory dephasing ($\gamma$)}

Increasing $\gamma$ from 0 to $10^5$ leaves the raw chain and paper schedule unchanged, as neither requires resources to wait for another branch. In contrast, heralded end-node pumping drops from $F=0.92$ to $F=0.25$ as its sacrificial copies accumulate dephasing during repeated heralding delays. The optimizer's schedules remain stable across the sweep, favouring optimistic schedules that avoid these storage intervals (Section~\ref{sec:disc:optimistic}).

\paragraph{Generation latency ($\tau_{\mathrm{emit}}$)}

Increasing $\tau_{\mathrm{emit}}$ at $\gamma=0$ reduces the rate for all schedules. The relative loss is largest for optimistic pumping, with a baseline latency of about $1\times L/c$, and smallest for heralded pumping, with about $9\times L/c$. The two cross near $\tau_{\mathrm{emit}}\approx0.01$, above which the larger fixed latency of heralded pumping makes it less sensitive to further increases.

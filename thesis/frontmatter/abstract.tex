Long-distance quantum networks require repeaters to distribute high-fidelity Bell pairs despite photon loss and operational noise. In half-repeater-graph- state (HRGS) all-photonic repeaters, entanglement purification can be applied during resource generation, after a single hop, or after an end-to-end pair is established. Selecting where to purify, how many independent copies to consume, and which circuit to apply is therefore a combinatorial scheduling problem.

This thesis formalises every fixed, non-adaptive purification schedule as a directed acyclic graph and evaluates it with one bottom-up pass that computes fidelity, success probability, latency, rate, and generation-resource cost. The evaluator reproduces the source protocol's three canonical fidelity benchmarks to four significant figures. On top of this model, the work implements structured baseline enumeration, a span-partition dynamic program, same-span pumping, and beam search. All returned candidates are structurally validated schedules evaluated by the same physical model.

At the source protocol's nominal configuration, the matched-cost searched schedule improves rate by $2.5\%$ over the hand-designed baseline. Allowing the search to use less than the reference budget yields a valid schedule at 60\% of that budget with a $52.8\%$ higher rate. More importantly, when inner-qubit error is introduced, the fixed baseline falls below the required fidelity while a searched schedule restores feasibility within the same resource budget. Additional experiments characterize noise sensitivity, timing effects, and the minimum searched resource budget as the chain grows.

These results demonstrate the value of systematic schedule selection within the implemented model. They do not establish global optimality: default pumping and beam search retain bounded frontiers, and a schedule not found is only absent from the families explored by the corresponding search.

\begin{abstract}

[ALTERNATIVE ABSTRACT]

All-photonic quantum repeaters based on half-repeater graph states (HRGS) allow entanglement purification to be applied at any stage of a multi-hop chain: during resource generation, after a single link, or at the end nodes. The Integrating paper demonstrates this flexibility with one hand-chosen schedule and identifies the search for a better one as an open problem. This thesis addresses that gap by providing a systematic framework for finding and comparing purification schedules under a fixed generation-resource budget.

Every fixed, non-adaptive schedule is represented as a directed acyclic graph and evaluated by a single bottom-up pass computing fidelity, rate, success probability, and resource cost. The evaluator reproduces the source paper's three canonical fidelity benchmarks to four significant figures. Three search tiers---structured baseline enumeration, a Pareto-multi-objective span-partition dynamic program, and bounded beam search---are built on top of this shared evaluator, so every returned schedule is a physically validated construction rather than a search-time approximation.

At the source paper's nominal $N=10$ configuration, the matched-cost searched schedule improves rate by $2.5\%$ over the hand-designed baseline at identical resource cost, and a budget-relaxed schedule achieves $52.8\%$ higher rate while using only $60\%$ of that budget. When inner-qubit error is introduced, the fixed baseline drops below the $F \ge 0.9$ fidelity floor, whereas a searched schedule restores feasibility within the same budget---a qualitative advantage that a rate percentage cannot capture. Additional experiments sweep noise level, memory decoherence, generation timing, and chain length $N$, and extract five named design findings. All results are existence claims within the searched schedule families, not proofs of global optimality.

\end{abstract}
